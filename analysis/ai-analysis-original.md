## Results interpreted

Across Load, Stress, Spike, and endurance, the harness completed 2,716/2,716
WF-03 lifecycles, issued 19,012 HTTP requests with zero failures, and canceled
all 2,716 created orders. Overall p95 was 4.1019 ms for Load, 3.9147 ms for
Stress, 3.9654 ms for Spike, and 4.377 ms for endurance. These aggregate values
come from different schedules, so their RPS and percentile values are not
capacity-equivalent comparisons.

The transactional steps were consistently slowest relative to other endpoints.
Checkout p95 ranged from 4.125 to 4.4698 ms; cancellation p95 ranged from 4.45
to 4.8544 ms. This makes them the first optimization candidates, but the data
does not prove either is a limiting bottleneck: absolute times were low, every
lifecycle succeeded, and no tested stage exposed a failure knee.

## Stress behavior

Stress increased the bounded input through 2, 5, 10, 15, and 20 VUs. The two
full 20-VU buckets delivered approximately 25.93 and 26.53 requests/s with p95
approximately 3.75 and 3.72 ms and zero failures. No latency escalation,
authentication failure, dropped lifecycle, or SQLite busy error was observed.
Therefore degradation was **not observed through the tested 20-VU input**.
This does not establish 20 VUs as maximum stable capacity because the plan did
not continue until failure or saturation.

## Spike and recovery

The 45-second 20-VU peak produced approximately 26.377778 requests/s, p95
3.5988 ms, and zero failures. Baseline p95 was 4.324 ms and recovery p95 4.038
ms; recovery returned to approximately three VUs with zero failures. The tested
spike recovered without an observed backlog or error cascade. The lower peak
p95 than baseline should not be interpreted as load improving the SUT; it can
reflect short windows, local scheduling/cache effects, and sample variation.

## Endurance behavior

At five VUs for 12 sustained minutes, all 713 workflows completed. First-half
versus second-half mean RPS was 6.6 versus 6.636111, while mean one-minute p95
was 4.45082 versus 4.085 ms. The order table grew linearly to 713 canceled rows
without observed latency drift or integrity error. This demonstrates a local
endurance point of 5 VUs for 12 minutes. A single midpoint screenshot (1.3%
CPU, 56.1 MB, 11 threads) cannot establish CPU averages or memory stability.

## Candidate regression thresholds for human review

These are proposed same-machine regression guardrails, not assignment facts or
measured capacity:

- workflow success rate at least 99%;
- HTTP failure rate below 1%;
- aggregate `http_req_duration` p95 below 10 ms;
- lifecycle-duration p95 below 1,500 ms;
- scenario throughput warning if a repeat with the same workload falls more
  than 20% below its recorded baseline without an intentional environment
  change.

The 10 ms and 1,500 ms limits add headroom above the largest observed aggregate
p95 (4.377 ms) and lifecycle p95 (989.8 ms). They are deliberately proposals;
repeat-run variability has not been quantified, so enforcing them as hard CI
gates now could create false alarms.

## Optimization hypotheses

1. Avoid the successful-login `UPDATE users` when `login_attempts` is already
   zero and `locked_until` is null. WF-03 authenticates every iteration, so this
   source-backed write is usually redundant and adds SQLite write serialization.
2. Replace cancellation's SELECT-then-UPDATE sequence with one conditional
   owner/state-scoped UPDATE and check `changes`. This can reduce a database
   round trip and close a race while preserving business rules if implemented
   carefully.
3. Experiment with SQLite WAL and a bounded busy timeout in a disposable branch.
   The current database reports delete journal mode and zero busy timeout, but
   the measured runs had no lock failures; this is resilience testing, not a
   proven necessary fix.
4. Evaluate a non-unique index on `users(email)` for login lookup as account
   volume grows. Current results with 22 runtime users do not demonstrate an
   email-lookup bottleneck; a unique index would also change registration
   semantics and needs separate data review.

No genuine SUT performance bug was confirmed by these runs. The strongest
conclusion is bounded: the tested workflows were correct and stable within the
specified local inputs, while maximum capacity and long-duration resource
behavior remain unknown.
